Welcome to Treaty 1 Territory

Treaty Relationships Today

Treaty relationship today in Canada are diverse, evolving, and remain legally,
socially, and culturally significant. These relationships are in both historic treaties,
signed mostly between 1701 and 1923, and modern treaties negotiated after 1975.

Importance of Respecting Indigenous Rights

Respecting Indigenous right is crucial for ensuring social justice,
preserving cultural heritage, promoting self-determination, and
fostering reconciliation and sustainable development.

Land Acknowledgements

Treaty 1 land acknowledgements recognize that we are on the ancestral
and unceded lands of the Anishinaabeg, Anishininewuk, Dakota Oyate, Denesuline
Nehethowuk and the Red River Métis.A land acknowledgement is a formal
statement recognizing the traditional territories of Indigenous peoples and
expressing respect for their enduring connection to the land.

Treaty 1 First Nations Communities

First Nations under Treaty 1 are primarily from the Anishinaabe (Saulteaux)
and Swampy Cree peoples. These communities include, among others:

  • Brokenhead Ojibway Nation
  • Rolling River First Nation
  • Lake St. Martin First Nation
  • Long Plain First Nation
  • Peguis First Nation
  • Dakota Plains Ojibway Nation

These communities are located throughout southern Manitoba, including
areas around Winnipeg, the Interlake region, and along rivers such as the
Red River and Lake Winnipeg. Each First Nation maintains governance through
its band council and operates community programs, education initiatives, and
cultural preservation efforts.

Treaty 1 Territory Acknowledgement:

We acknowledge that we are on Treaty 1 Territory, the traditional
lands of the Anishinaabe, Cree, Oji-Cree, Dakota and Dene Peoples
and the homeland of the Metis Nation.
